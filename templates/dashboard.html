Dataset & Experimental Design

Dataset

UCI ML Repository -- "Predict Students' Dropout and Academic Success" (Realinho et al., 2022)

  • 4,424 student records, 35 original features + 7 engineered
  • Binary target: Dropout (n=1,421, 32.1%) vs Non-Dropout (n=3,003)
  • Class imbalance ratio 1:2.11, handled via scale_pos_weight

Two-Stage Digital Twin

Staged assessment simulating progressive counselor decision-making:

  • Stage 1 (27 features): demographics + Sem 1 -- early warning
  • Stage 2 (40 features): + Sem 2 + macro -- refined prediction
  • Data-driven intervention modeling with Sem 2 outcome predictor

Understanding Your Dashboard

This page explains what each part of your dashboard means and how you can use it to stay on track.

What Is Your Risk Score?

Your risk score shows how likely you are to face academic difficulties. It ranges from 0% to 100%.

  • Low (0-30%) -- You are on track. Keep doing what you're doing.
  • Moderate (30-50%) -- Stay consistent. There are some areas to watch.
  • High (50-75%) -- You may need support. Consider reaching out for help.
  • Critical (75-100%) -- Please take action soon. Talk to your advisor or support services.

Your score is calculated based on your academic performance, financial situation, and engagement. It updates as your data changes.

What Is Your Stress Score?

Your stress score is a number from 0 to 10 that estimates how much pressure you may be under. It considers:

  • Your financial situation -- Are your fees paid? Do you have outstanding debt?
  • Your academic results -- How many subjects have you passed or failed?
  • Your engagement -- Are you attending your exams? Or missing them?
  • Your grades -- How close are you to the passing mark?

A score above 5 means you are under significant pressure. If your stress is high, the actions section will suggest ways to reduce it.

How Do We Suggest Actions For You?

We look at your academic progress and identify the areas where you may need the most help. Based on what is affecting you the most, we suggest specific steps:

  • Academic support -- Improve your grades and complete more assignments
  • Stress support -- Manage your workload and seek counseling if needed
  • Financial support -- Explore scholarships, fee waivers, or payment plans
  • Engagement support -- Attend more classes and attempt all your exams

The suggestions are personalized to your situation -- they change based on your specific risk factors.

What Is The Future Simulation?

The simulation shows you how your risk might change over the next 6 weeks depending on what you do:

  • No Support -- What might happen if nothing changes
  • With Mentoring -- If you receive regular academic guidance
  • With Financial Aid -- If your financial pressure is reduced

This helps you see the potential benefit of getting support. It's a projection, not a guarantee -- but it's based on patterns from real academic data.

How To Use Your Dashboard

  • Step 1: Check your risk score at the top of your dashboard
  • Step 2: Look at why you have this score -- these are the main factors affecting you
  • Step 3: Review the suggested actions -- these are personalized to your situation
  • Step 4: Try different options in the simulation to see how your risk could change
  • Step 5: Take steps to improve -- seek support where needed

Remember: your risk score is not a final judgment. It's a tool to help you identify areas for improvement and take action early.

How This System Works

This system helps identify students who may be at risk of dropping out and suggests actions to support them.

What is the Risk Score?

Each student receives a risk score from 0% to 100%. This represents the likelihood that the student may drop out.

  • Low (0-30%) -- On track. No immediate action needed.
  • Moderate (30-50%) -- Some concerns. Monitor periodically.
  • High (50-75%) -- Significant risk. Schedule a counseling session.
  • Critical (75-100%) -- Urgent. Immediate intervention recommended.

What is the Stress Indicator?

A score from 0 to 10 estimating academic pressure based on financial situation, performance, engagement, and grades. Students above 5 should be prioritized for support.

How Are Recommendations Generated?

The system identifies each student's top risk factors and groups them into categories (Academic, Financial, Engagement, Stress). Based on the highest-impact categories, it suggests specific counseling actions.

How to Use This System

  • Go to Students tab and filter by "At Risk"
  • Click a student for detailed risk profile and SHAP explanation
  • Review recommendations and timeline simulation
  • Use the recommended intervention to guide your decision

Methodology and Experimental Framework

Overview

An explainable AI-based digital twin framework for early academic risk prediction and counseling decision support. The system combines XGBoost classification with SHAP explainability and data-driven intervention simulation to identify at-risk students after Semester 1 and recommend targeted interventions. Based on UCI ML dataset (Realinho et al., 2022; 4,424 students, 35 features).

Method

Classification

  • Two-stage XGBoost classifier (Stage 1: 27 features, Stage 2: 40 features)
  • Baselines: Logistic Regression, Voting Ensemble (LR+XGB)
  • Imbalance handling via scale_pos_weight (ratio ~2.11)
  • 5-fold stratified cross-validation for stability estimation

Explainability & Intervention

  • SHAP TreeExplainer for global and local feature attribution
  • Category-based recommendation mapping (Academic, Financial, Engagement, Stress)
  • Data-driven intervention effects from graduate/dropout trajectory analysis
  • Sem 2 outcome predictor (MultiOutput XGBRegressor, R2=0.85)
Key Contributions
  • A two-stage digital twin framework that evolves with incoming semester data, improving AUC from 89.8% to 93.0%
  • SHAP-based category aggregation that maps model explanations to actionable counseling strategies
  • Data-driven weekly simulation replacing heuristic intervention rules, with stability constraints (delta caps, monotonic enforcement)
Results

Performance Summary

  • Stage 1 XGBoost: AUC = 0.898, Accuracy = 86.3%
  • Stage 2 XGBoost: AUC = 0.930, Accuracy = 88.3%
  • 5-fold CV: 89.6% +/- 0.77% (Stage 1), 92.1% +/- 0.77% (Stage 2)
  • Stage 2 improves over Stage 1 by +3.2% AUC

Top Predictive Features

  • Approval_rate_sem2 (|SHAP| = 1.21)
  • Stress_indicator (|SHAP| = 0.47)
  • Tuition fees up to date (|SHAP| = 0.43)
  • Course (|SHAP| = 0.37)
Limitations
  • Observational dataset -- no causal claims can be made regarding interventions
  • Simulated intervention effects are data-derived approximations, not validated through randomized trials
  • Stress indicator is a domain-informed heuristic, not a clinically validated instrument
  • Single-institution Portuguese university data may limit cross-cultural generalizability
Future Work
  • Prospective validation with real intervention outcomes from partner institutions
  • Temporal modeling via sequence architectures (LSTM, Transformer)
  • Causal inference integration (propensity score matching, DoWhy framework)
  • Personalized intervention optimization using reinforcement learning
  • Multi-institution and cross-cultural generalization studies
